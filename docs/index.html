01 / FrameworkThe proposed method

How words and
deeds meet

Researchers want to measure traits, beliefs or priorities that organizational records cannot directly reveal. The proposal uses AI to identify language about a chosen trait, then predict a separately chosen behavior. Predictions for observations held out of model fitting become a candidate measure. Researchers specify the construct and evaluation; AI readers supply inspectable semantic judgments.

Figure 1 in words

  1. 1

    Characterize the corpus

    Discover topics across the entire corpus without using the focal trait to define them. A common catalogue lets researchers compare firms and follow changes within each firm.

  2. 2

    Decontextualize topics

    Three AI readers judge descriptors, then review shared passages in their own histories. An unweighted two-of-three vote retains a topic judged to reflect the trait. Readers can disagree about whether a passage only describes the firm’s setting.

  3. 3

    Synthesize the proxies

    The behavioral proxy is the observed decision. Predict it using construct-relevant narrative features and any covariates specified for the application. The held-out predicted value is the synthesized proxy. The signed residual is observed minus predicted behavior. Its interpretation requires separate evidence.

  4. 4

    Validate the proxy

    Convergent validity asks whether the score tracks both the narrative and behavioral measures. Incremental validity asks whether the score adds explanatory power for an external outcome beyond both measures together.

Prediction / Model specification

What did the model actually use?

Proposed framework

Each retained topic’s density measures emphasis and its sentiment measures tone. The target, feature transformations, and any covariates are specified for each application. Alternative AI selections can be carried through learning and assessment.

Delivered CEO model

321 transformed topic densities and one aggregate sentiment feature. The prediction models omitted firm controls and fixed effects. The target was contemporaneous R&D divided by assets.

03 / EvidencePrediction and downstream association

What did the
score predict?

The models predict a firm’s investment. Their scores also track dividend decisions after adjusting for actual R&D. The final appendix extends that comparison to a narrative proxy. These conditional associations leave open how much of the signal belongs to the CEO.

Both models scored 29,506 firm-years. Cross-fitted R² compares predictions with the 29,191 available targets. Five folds kept firms linked through a shared CEO together, so each prediction came from a model trained on other groups.

When assets were positive, the study set missing R&D to zero. This applied to 42.7% of target-available rows. The firm–CEO holdout applies to supervised prediction. All folds used the same pre-existing topic catalogue.

Final appendix / Table A13

What changes when both component proxies enter?

Eight additional dividend models include the narrative proxy and actual R&D controls together. The synthesized-proxy coefficient stays negative and significant at the 1% level in all eight. In the inside-debt specification, the coefficients are −0.146 for Ridge and −0.141 for XGBoost. The narrative-proxy coefficients are negative but statistically insignificant.

This is an author-supplied appendix result. The repository’s existing connected reproduction covers 25 models; it does not yet reproduce the eight Table A13 extensions. Significance after adjustment is evidence of a conditional association, and does not by itself establish superior measurement.

What the reproduction repeated

Replayed
Checked the saved topic identities and ordering, then recomputed selections from saved reader judgments. The reproduction began with the existing topic catalogue. Historical row alignment depends on the supplied ordering.
Refitted
Trained Ridge and XGBoost again from fresh downloads, using the selected topics and the original grouped folds.
Estimated
Matched the new scores to financial inputs and ran all 25 dividend specifications in Stata, including the 16 models that use a predicted score.
04 / Open testsResearch agenda

What would change
the interpretation?

The final appendix reports prediction and dividend-policy evidence, including adjustment for both component proxies. Independent construct validation and downstream comparisons across AI configurations remain the next tests.

  1. Can people recognize the same evidence?

    Calibrate AI votes against independent human judgments, including disputed cases. Record model versions, prompts, source context and votes. Persistent disagreement with humans or large shifts across settings would weaken confidence in selection.

  2. Does synthesis add anything?

    Check convergence with both component measures and with an independent criterion. Extend the Table A13 adjustment with common-sample performance comparisons at comparable model flexibility. No added performance would weaken the incremental claim.

  3. Do different AI judgments change the result?

    Refit all twelve retained topic selections and compare them with all topics, conventional feature selection, prediction averaging, and selection-informed Ridge penalties. Preserve the same held-out groups to test whether AI judgments improve measurement.

  4. Does the signal follow the person?

    Separate variation across CEOs, firms and industries. If common industry shocks or persistent firm differences explain most of the signal, attributing it to personal risk preference becomes harder.

  5. Does the score move when expected?

    Preregister events that change firms’ decision environments and specify the expected score response in advance. Flat or opposite movements would challenge that interpretation.

  6. Does the method travel?

    The proposal reports an additional political-ideology trial using donation records. That trial’s results and reproduction package are not included here. A documented second application would test what transfers beyond the CEO case.

The coordinator dispatches four stages and preserves their inputs and outputs so an interrupted run can resume. Researchers choose the construct, specification and interpretation.
