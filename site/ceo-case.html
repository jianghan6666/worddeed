Preliminary validation

CEOs’ risk preference

We use CEOs’ language in conference calls to develop the narrative proxy and R&D intensity as the behavioral proxy. This demo study examines the feasibility of the proposed workflow.

01

Characterizing the Corpus

A fixed topic catalogue describes recurring themes in earnings calls. The case reuses this representation to compare speech across firms and over time.

02

Topic decontextualization

Three LLM agents first classify topic descriptors, then review common source passages within their own histories. The task is selection and interpretation: whether a topic is subject-specific or context-dependent.

03

Synthesize the narrative and behavioral proxies

We use XGBoost and Ridge regression to predict R&D intensity from selected topic densities and overall sentiment. Each score comes from a model fitted without that observation’s connected firm–CEO group. The behavioral outcome reconstructed from the narrative evidence serves as the synthesized proxy.

04

Validate the synthesized proxy

The synthesized proxy is used in an adaptation of Caliskan and Doukas (2015). Higher scores are associated with lower odds of dividend payment after adjustment for actual R&D and specified CEO and firm characteristics.

Implications for measurement

The framework strives to identify the common variation between the behavioral and narrative proxies as a synthesized proxy. Retained judgments make the measurement choices inspectable; comparing alternative selections through the models remains development work.

The score remains a candidate measure.

Firm circumstances may shape both speech and investment. The narrative-only benchmark is weakly related to the learned scores, and the topic set was chosen after preliminary predictive comparisons. The dividend associations establish neither a causal effect nor a psychological trait. Independent validation and comparisons across alternative selections remain necessary.

Method and evidence notes

The application uses 321 selected topic densities and one aggregate sentiment feature with Ridge and XGBoost. Firm controls and fixed effects do not enter the prediction model. The later dividend regressions include their own controls.

The topic catalogue comes from an earlier model trained on text components with available donation-based ideology data. Holding groups out of supervised fitting does not make the earlier representation or performance-informed topic choice independent of the evaluated data.

The final appendix also reports models that adjust for both the narrative-only benchmark and actual R&D, plus correlations among the proxies. These author-reported results fall outside the recorded connected reproduction. The source guide points to the specifications and numerical evidence.
