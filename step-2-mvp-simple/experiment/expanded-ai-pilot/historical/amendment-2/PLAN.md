## Why this study is worthwhile

Step 2 connects the Step 1 topic catalogue to the substantive risk-relevance rule and the Step 3 input list. Its value is a transparent selection with source evidence and preserved disagreement. The expanded study asks how dependent that selection is on implementation choices. The [exploratory pilot](../comparison-2026-10-19/comparison.md) found **71/354 topics (20.1%) changing category** and **54–95 selected topics** across conditions. This is a reason to examine sensitivity, not an accuracy estimate or an isolated model/effort effect; the pilot's differing wrappers and audit limitations remain disclosed.

[SMJ (2026), Table 3 and §§3.4–3.5](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.70023), printed pp.711–720 / PDF pp.13–22, provides the methodological rationale: model/prompt choices can alter labels and downstream findings; higher first-stage accuracy need not yield better downstream inference. The paper also warns against selecting configurations for preferred downstream results. We adapt its seven strategy families. The **84-trial allocation**, effort factor, repeated triads and common source review are project design choices, not a sample size or procedure shown optimal by that paper.

| Decision question | Planned evidence |
|---|---|
| Does the selected list depend on model, prompt or requested effort? | Matched comparisons across the 42 configurations, preserving topic-level changes and all contrasts |
| Does the same procedure repeat consistently? | Two fresh ensemble trials per configuration, with reader and majority disagreement reported separately |
| Are topic interpretations and labels supported by retained text? | Sealed initial records followed by the common source review, exact passage references, reasons, confidence and retained/revised/unresolved decisions for every topic |
| What can Step 3 rely on provisionally, and what needs inspection? | Both topic lists, minority votes, missingness, stable and changing IDs, source limitations and the complete sensitivity record |

**Success means answering these questions transparently, not reaching a target level of agreement or a preferred number of selected topics.** Stability would support reproducibility within the tested settings; disagreement would identify fragile decisions or possible codebook ambiguities. Neither outcome proves semantic correctness. The same 354 topics recur throughout: 84 trials do not represent 252 independent topic samples or establish generalization to another corpus. Two repetitions provide descriptive evidence with limited precision.

Use the [prespecified analysis](#prespecified-ai-only-analysis) to report all configurations. Do not choose a new primary classifier using greatest agreement, highest confidence, largest selection or favorable downstream fit. Source citations make decisions inspectable but do not by themselves validate them; Stage A–B differences do not isolate a causal benefit of text access. Human-reference evaluation remains a later plan, consistent with the distinction between agreement and reference-relative accuracy in [PNAS (2023), methods PDF pp.3–6](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2305016120). The current provisional handoff remains available.

The reduced design is **2 models × 7 strategies × 3 efforts × 2 repetitions = 84 trials**. Revision 9 records the Operator's amendment and renewed execution authorization and [seat/context procedure](SEAT_PLAN.md). The [technical appendix](../../TECHNICAL_APPENDIX.md#why-step-2-and-the-expanded-study-are-worthwhile) gives the fuller rationale and claim limits.

### Reasons, confidence and source traceback

The [output contract](OUTPUT_CONTRACT.md) specifies immutable initial judgments, separate reviewed judgments and a linked source-evidence table. The [Stage A output addendum](READER_OUTPUT_ADDENDUM.md) and [Stage B review prompt](SOURCE_REVIEW_PROMPT.md) require label reasons and high/medium/low confidence with its own reason at both stages. Confidence describes evidence sufficiency/codebook ambiguity, not risk severity or probability of correctness. Abstention/failure has null confidence. All seven conditions use the same staged instructions; the historical 12 runs are not backfilled.

Self-reported confidence, two-of-three agreement and repeated-run stability are different diagnostics. [ICLR (2024)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6733cf15e10e2cd1d59af033c3bb8507-Abstract-Conference.html), published abstract read 20 October 2026, report verbalized overconfidence and imperfect calibration in their evaluation. Our ordinal rubric is a design choice, not a calibrated instrument. Report confidence and missingness descriptively; it neither changes the majority nor identifies the most accurate model. Confidence elicitation can itself affect answers, another reason to keep the old pilot separate.

The [direct source inspection](SOURCE_TRACE_INSPECTION.md) recovered **three saved representative passages for every topic: 1,062 entries**, all matched exactly to the retained corpus. It also verified the `orig_row` join for **all 1,775,355 CEO-subset rows** against seven identity fields with zero mismatches. Text is recoverable, but it is masked/processed speech, and the saved topic representatives are not all CEO speech. Preserve duplicate source matches and delivered attribution. Use the same three saved passages in their saved order for every condition, without selecting examples after seeing labels. They illustrate a topic rather than exhaust its contents. Missing CEO attribution does not make a topic irrelevant. The review packet must display these limits and retain all candidate matches; it must not represent every passage as a CEO quotation. Historical density binding remains a separate limitation. This main review uses saved representatives, not newly ranked density-based samples.

Freeze the passage packet and its hashes before collection, but withhold its contents from readers until their complete initial response is sealed. All three readers' initial attempts must be sealed or explicitly recorded as missing before Stage B release. Continue each reader's own standalone chat with the same model/effort; readers never see peers' outputs at either stage. Read the full frozen passage set through the agreed packet procedure. Use the retained native feasibility evidence and frozen wrapper; do not silently truncate text, substitute summaries or switch to independent per-topic contexts. No additional readiness campaign is required. Use the ratified starting layout of 18 Stage B pages (17 × 20 topics, then 14), in the same reader chat, with exact evidence, saved page records and a common continuation/compaction policy. Retain the frozen transport and recovery procedure, without artificial time/token caps.

## Recommendation and published basis

Use a fully crossed **2-model × 7-prompt × 3-effort design (Medium, High, Xhigh)**, with **2 fresh ensemble trials per configuration and 3 independent readers per trial**. That is **42 configurations, 84 two-stage ensemble trials and 252 fresh standalone reader sessions**. Each reader produces an initial and a reviewed response: **504 planned reader-stage responses; 89,208 topic judgments per stage, or 178,416 across both stages**. These are logical response/judgment counts, not a guarantee of exactly that many API calls or independent observations. A second stage in the same reader does not double the number of independent trials/readers. Failures remain in the scheduled denominator. The previous 12 trials are historical comparisons, not extra replications.

[Strategic Management Journal (2026), 47(3), 699–725](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.70023) provides the direct precedent: five models crossed with seven prompting strategies, evaluated against 250 human-consensus campaign labels, followed by prompt and downstream sensitivity analyses. Their Table 3's self-consistency instruction requests repeated evaluation inside one call; it does not create independently retained reader votes. We adopt the controlled model/prompt comparison, while deferring reference-relative accuracy. We adapt all seven named strategy families in their Table 3 to the risk-relevance task; this is not a replication of their full experiment. **Locators:** §§3.1–3.4, PDF pp.10–16; Table 3 p.13; prompt sensitivity pp.16–22. The [methods comparison](../ANNOTATION_METHODS.md) and [source ledger](../ANNOTATION_METHOD_SOURCES.json) identify the saved published version and reading limits.

[PNAS (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2305016120), methods PDF pp.3–6, distinguishes repeated-call agreement from accuracy against trained annotators. That distinction governs our endpoints. Two ensemble repetitions are our starting descriptive design, **not a literature-established sample size or power guarantee**. Six readers within a configuration allow two disjoint votes; they do not provide six independently replicated three-reader ensembles. Report the observed spread without claiming equivalence when differences are small.

## Seven SMJ strategy adaptations

The seven templates apply to **Stage A**. Each starts with the same [prospective P0 classification text](prompts/P0-direct.md); P1–P6 append one treatment. All use the same [confidence/output addendum](READER_OUTPUT_ADDENDUM.md). The shared risk definition, descriptor catalogue, output schema and exclusions remain fixed. **Stage B uses one common [source-review prompt](SOURCE_REVIEW_PROMPT.md)** after initial sealing, explicitly permitting supplied passages. It is not an eighth strategy or an additional independent trial.

| ID / SMJ strategy | Task-specific instruction | Published basis and adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| [P0: Zero-shot](prompts/P0-direct.md) | Apply the shared codebook directly, without labeled examples. | SMJ Table 3 zero-shot condition. |
| [P6: Few-shot](prompts/P6-examples.md) | Add six frozen fictional examples, balanced across the two labels. | SMJ Table 3 few-shot condition. Our examples and their labels are AI-authored illustrations, not human references. |
| [P1: Role-based](prompts/P1-role.md) | Add a management-research annotator role without changing evidence or the relevance threshold. | SMJ Table 3 role-based condition. |
| [P2: Chain of thought (CoT)](prompts/P2-criteria.md) | Apply the same inclusion routes and generic-background exclusion once in an explicit order. | Task-specific adaptation of SMJ Table 3 ordered reasoning; return a concise justification, not a reasoning trace. |
| [P4: Self-consistency](prompts/P4-repeat.md) | Request three complete evaluations from fixed perspectives within one reader, then one internal-majority label. | Adapts SMJ Table 3's within-call procedure. Each evaluation uses the full rule; perspectives are not separate criterion votes. |
| [P5: Tree of thought (ToT)](prompts/P5-tree.md) | Examine direct-risk, decision-scenario and generic-background paths; synthesize under the shared codebook. | Adapts SMJ Table 3 alternative paths. Paths are not votes or an external search-tree implementation. |
| [P3: Reflection on search trees (RoT)](prompts/P3-reflection.md) | Compare support for both classifications, reflect on evidence strength and uncertainty, then decide. | Adapts SMJ Table 3 reflection, including path confidence. Do not fabricate supporting arguments; no external tree search. |

The strategy set now matches the seven families in [SMJ (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.70023), Table 3, printed p.711 / PDF p.13: zero-shot, few-shot, role-based, CoT, self-consistency, ToT and RoT. These are risk-task adaptations, not verbatim prompts or a replication of the paper's full experiment. That study compared five models on 250 human-consensus cases and requested a final binary output; our two-model design adds reasons, confidence, repeated three-reader ensembles and sequential source review, without human-reference accuracy evaluation. Table 2 describes broader methods; the within-call implementation in Table 3 governs our self-consistency adaptation.

Prompt IDs remain stable where possible, so the table is ordered by the paper's families rather than numeric ID. Revision 7 replaces the separate P5 self-audit with ToT, expands P3 into RoT and revises P4 to three fixed perspectives. The revision-6 templates and 441-trial schedule remain preserved in [PR #13](https://github.com/joyzhzh/narrative-behavior-measurement/pull/13) and Git history; they must not be substituted under revision-7 IDs. The codebook is unchanged. Prospective P0 now clarifies that the initial majority is retained separately and final voting follows source review.

P4's internal majority yields one reader vote; it does not create three independent readers or nine votes per trial. P5 synthesizes paths without voting on separate criteria: either supported inclusion route can qualify. P3 requests reflection on supported cases, not invented counterarguments. These are instructions whose internal execution cannot be verified from final answers. ToT/RoT do not implement external search algorithms. All conditions return the same concise evidence-based output, without hidden reasoning traces. Requested reasoning effort remains a separate experimental factor.

The fixed six P6 examples are AI-authored and are not reference labels, held-out evaluation items or evidence of accuracy. No real topic ID, original pilot vote or downstream outcome is used as an example. Their balance imposes no target balance on actual labels. Their number and content are adaptations; the SMJ table uses its own three examples. [PLOS One (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0354757), §§5–6, additionally motivates separating codebook content, examples and framing, but does not establish the effectiveness of our templates.

The study estimates effects of these seven frozen wordings, including their length/content differences, not universal effects of seven distinct cognitive mechanisms. The papers justify controlled sensitivity comparisons, not the 84-trial sample size or superiority of any strategy. The common Stage B review remains our extension; paired changes combine text access, reconsideration and anchoring rather than isolating a causal effect of text access.

## Prespecified AI-only analysis

The target is the complete, fixed 354-topic catalogue. **Stage B reviewed labels are the primary handoff endpoint; Stage A is the separately reported initial baseline.** At each stage, compare P1–P6 to P0 within model/effort, models under the same template/effort, and Medium–High, High–Xhigh and Medium–Xhigh within model/template. Match repeat identities and retain all contrasts rather than averaging away reversals. Prompt grouping and capacity-dependent overlap can confound comparisons with time, load and session environment; report actual batch/start/end information and material resource delays. Original blocks no longer denote simultaneous execution periods. Stage B template contrasts describe the whole sequential procedure following each Stage A template, since the earlier context remains. Two ensemble replications per cell provide limited precision. Primary outputs are:

- **Coverage and protocol compliance:** scheduled/attempted/completed readers, resolved/abstained/failed topics, exact evidence-citation validity and observable generic-reason deviations, by cell and block.
- **Change after source review:** initial versus reviewed label transitions, retained/revised/newly-resolved/unresolved counts, confidence transitions and final-list changes within the same reader/trial. Record passage support, conflicting evidence, attribution limits and reasons for every change or retention. Report denominators for jointly resolved pairs and all missing-stage combinations. Change or increased confidence is not evidence of improved accuracy.
- **Self-reported confidence:** high/medium/low distributions and missingness by model, template, effort and label; descriptive association with disagreements and repeated-label changes. Retain the confidence reasons and minority reports. No calibration, confidence-weighted vote or confidence-based accuracy ranking is claimed.
- **Repeatability:** selected counts for each reader and each of the two ensemble trials; the one within-cell ensemble pair disagreement; per-topic selection counts out of six reader attempts and two trial attempts, with missingness alongside them. These frequencies describe the procedure, not the probability that a topic is truly relevant.
- **Model/prompt/effort sensitivity:** paired label changes, selected-set Jaccard, and maps of consistently selected, consistently excluded and changing topics, both within each effort and across the complete grid. Match blocks and retain results by block. A reader's 354 labels share context and cannot count as 354 independent run replicates; neither can overlapping pairs of runs.
- **Agreement with prevalence visible:** report the 2×2 paired counts, raw agreement, positive and negative agreement. If `a` is both relevant, `d` both irrelevant, and `b,c` discordant, positive agreement is `2a/(2a+b+c)` and negative agreement is `2d/(2d+b+c)`. Undefined denominators remain undefined. Jaccard is `a/(a+b+c)`. Use jointly resolved topics for these comparisons and report that denominator beside coverage over all 354. High agreement caused by common exclusions should be visible.

Report coverage, confidence and evidence support separately for each stage. For principal ensemble repeatability comparisons, require all three votes resolved at the relevant stage in each compared trial and report the denominator. Separately show operational majorities with two matching votes and a missing third. Show paired changes and descriptive ranges; neither 89,208 judgments per stage nor 178,416 across both stages are independent observations. Source-review changes combine passage access, a second examination and prior-answer anchoring; there is no randomized no-passage re-review control. No causal improvement claim follows. This study covers the fixed descriptors and three saved representatives per topic, with only two ensemble trials per cell. Additional passages, future catalogues, providers and releases require separate evidence. Any extra repetitions follow a declared precision question and balanced amendment.

Additional AI reason audits are outside this reduced schedule. With two selected models, two auditors of different listed models that both differ from the producing model cannot be assigned. No extra audit chats are scheduled or counted; any future audit requires a separately specified design.

## Assessing ensembles without inventing extra experiments

Compare each final homogeneous Stage B majority with its three constituent reviewed outputs.
